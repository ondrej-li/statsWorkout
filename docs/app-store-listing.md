## Description — English (3345 / 4000)

```
StatsWorkout turns the activity already stored in Apple Health into statistics you can actually read.

Pick a period — the last 24 hours, a single day, a calendar week, a month, a quarter, a year or any custom range — and see:
• steps for the whole period
• distance covered
• elevation gained
• time spent exercising
• number of activities

Every period comes with a total, an average, the best bucket and a comparison against the previous period or the same period last year. A month that is still running is compared with the same elapsed days of the previous one, so the numbers never flatter you.

ALL DATA OR JUST WORKOUTS
Switch between "All data" — everything Apple Health monitors continuously, including the walk to the shop — and "Activities", which counts only what was recorded inside a workout. Same charts, same periods, one tap apart.

CHARTS BUILT FOR FINGERS
Pinch to zoom into a shorter stretch, drag sideways to pan, and tap a bar or the progress line to read the exact value for that hour, day, week or month. The progress chart shows your running total against the previous period, so you can see at a glance whether you are ahead.

EVERY WORKOUT, OPENED UP
Tap a workout to see it the way the Health app shows it: the route on a map, duration, distance, average pace, elevation, steps, active energy, average and maximum heart rate, and per-kilometre or per-mile splits.

Workouts also get a name you would use yourself — "Your morning walk around Brtnice", "Your night run around Okříšky". The time of day comes from when you started, the place from the centre of the recorded route, and the name appears in the workout list too.

SHARE YOUR PROGRESS
One tap turns the screen into a card for Instagram, Facebook, Messenger or WhatsApp: the period you picked, the filter you are on, the chart and every total. Workouts share their own card, with the shape of the route and the numbers that came with it. Both are 1080 × 1350, the shape those apps crop to without cutting anything off.

WIDGETS THAT MATCH YOUR DASHBOARD
Add a widget in any size and set it up the way you set up the app: the period (down to a custom range), how many periods back, all data or workouts only, which baseline to compare against, and which metrics to show. The size decides how many fit — one number on the small widget, three on the medium, five on the large — so a widget never looks crowded, and each one gets a chart of the period.

TEN LANGUAGES
English, Czech, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese, Japanese, Korean and Simplified Chinese. Follow your device, or pick one in Settings. Dates, numbers and workout names follow the language you choose.

THE DETAILS
• units follow your preference — kilometres or miles, metres or feet
• calendar-aware weeks, months, quarters and years, including daylight-saving days
• hourly resolution for day and 24-hour views
• dark and light mode, Dynamic Type, VoiceOver labels on every chart
• no account, no sign-up, no subscription

PRIVACY
StatsWorkout asks Apple Health for read-only access to steps, distances, flights climbed, active energy, workouts and workout routes. Every calculation happens on your device. There is no account, no analytics, no advertising, no third-party SDK and no server of ours that ever receives your health data.

Requires iOS 26 or later. iPhone and iPad.
```

## Review notes (1931 / 4000)

```
StatsWorkout reads steps and workouts from Apple Health. There is no login and no account, so a
reviewer needs nothing but the app itself:

1. Launch the app. On first launch it asks for Health access; the sheet is the standard iOS one.
   Access can be declined - the dashboard then explains how to enable it in iOS Settings.
2. To see the interface with no Health data at all, open Settings -> Data source -> Use demo data.
   Demo activity is generated on the device from a fixed seed and is labelled in the UI.
3. Tap the period chips (24h, Day, Week, Month, Quarter, Year) and the All data / Activities switch
   above the charts to see how the numbers are derived.
4. Pinch a chart to zoom, then tap a bar to read that bucket's exact value.
5. Open the Activities tab and tap a workout. The detail screen shows the route Apple Health
   recorded for that workout; routes are a separate Health permission (Workout Routes), and a
   workout without one says so and offers a button to grant that access.
6. Tap the share button on the dashboard (or on a workout) to see the card that goes to social apps;
   it is rendered on device from the numbers already on screen and shared as a PNG.
7. To see the widget: long-press the Home Screen, tap Edit (or +), choose StatsWorkout and pick a
   size. Its settings offer the same period, scope, comparison and metric choices as the app. The
   widget reads Apple Health directly (the same permission the app already has) and falls back to the
   app's local cache, so it shows numbers once Health access has been granted.
8. Settings -> Language switches the interface language. The app ships ten languages and follows
   the device by default; the choice applies after a restart, as the footer says.

No health data leaves the device: there is no server component, no analytics and no third-party SDK.
The privacy policy and support pages are at https://stats-workout.ondralinek.cz/.
```

## Localised metadata beyond English and Czech

Only those two listings exist today. Adding another store localisation needs a subtitle, promotional
text, description, keywords and What's New in that language. The app's own catalogs in
`Resources/L10n/` are a good source for the wording, and the capture script can take another locale
by adding one line to its `case "$locale"` block.

## Export compliance

`ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption` is already `false` in `Supporting/Info.plist`, so App Store Connect
does not ask.
